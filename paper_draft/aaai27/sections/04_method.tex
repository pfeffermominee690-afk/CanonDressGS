\section{Method}

\subsection{Overview}

The method separates spatial garment representation from reference conditioning. Multi-view teachers first instantiate garment-specific residual endpoints on the fixed canonical Gaussian support. A deterministic centered SVD converts the five seen endpoints into a frozen mean field and a $K=4$ explicit basis. At prediction time, a frozen feature extractor and deterministic set pooling summarize target-disjoint references; a small coefficient head predicts four standardized coordinates. The corresponding raw coefficient composes a canonical residual, after which the existing avatar deformation and renderer consume the target pose and camera.

Only the Ours-v2 coefficient branch is the proposed candidate in this section. B6 and B7 are hard-lookup baselines, while M3 and M4 are contract-matched causal controls. None is a component of the proposed inference path. Historical A6 is evidence about the rejected pairwise term and is not an Ours-v2 result.

\subsection{Multi-view Teacher Residual Optimization}

For each seen garment $g$, an independent \emph{UnboundedGaussianDeltaField} is initialized at physical zero on the original canonical support. It directly parameterizes position, log-scaling, axis-angle rotation, opacity-logit, and degree-zero spherical-harmonic residuals on the frozen garment-trainable support; the remaining spherical-harmonic residual channel is present in the six-channel contract and fixed to zero in this teacher implementation. The base Gaussian state is not optimized.

The four views are visited in a fixed round-robin order for 1,200 teacher optimizer steps. Each step composes the current canonical residual with the base, deforms/renders it at the selected target pose and camera, and applies the frozen capacity-oracle objective: garment RGB, foreground alpha, new-silhouette alpha, boundary RGB, protected RGB/alpha, and a small stability term. Target RGB and masks therefore supervise teacher optimization, but they never enter the later coefficient-prediction forward. After optimization, a frozen protected-region guard zeros every residual channel on the protected Gaussian set. The resulting $\mathcal{R}_g^{\star}$ is stored in bound-normalized six-channel form. Teacher construction is an onboarding prerequisite and an optimization upper bound, not proof of reference inference.

\subsection{Explicit Canonical Residual Basis}

Let $D$ divide each residual channel by its preregistered positive channel bound, and let $\mathbf r_g$ be the flattened concatenation of $D(\mathcal{R}_g^{\star})$. From the five seen teachers, we form
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathbf r}=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{seen}}}\mathbf r_g,
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathbf r}_g=\mathbf r_g-\bar{\mathbf r}.
\end{equation}
A deterministic SVD of the centered matrix fixes component signs by the largest-magnitude pivot. Unflattening the first $K$ right-singular vectors produces one explicit per-Gaussian field per component and channel. Five centered endpoints have rank at most four; the selected $K=4$ is the complete centered rank, not a strong compression setting.

Writing $\mu_{\mathcal R}$ for the unnormalized mean field and $B$ for the corresponding physical residual components, canonical reconstruction is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\mathcal{R}(\mathbf c)=\mu_{\mathcal R}+B\mathbf c.}
\end{equation}
The basis, mean, channel bounds, teacher coordinates, and coefficient normalization are frozen before coefficient-predictor fitting. O07 is excluded from every one of these operations.

\subsection{Reference Feature Aggregation}

The frozen image backbone produces a 128-channel spatial feature map for each reference. Clothing-mask weighted mean and masked maximum pooling are concatenated to form $\phi_j\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$. Given validity values $v_j$, the adapter computes
\begin{equation}
\phi_{\mathrm{mean}}=\frac{\sum_j v_j\phi_j}{\sum_j v_j},
\qquad
\phi_{\mathrm{max}}=\max_{j:v_j>0}\phi_j,
\end{equation}
and concatenates them as $\Phi(\mathcal{S}_t)=[\phi_{\mathrm{mean}},\phi_{\mathrm{max}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{512}$. This operation is deterministic, permutation invariant, and validity aware. Its only image-derived inputs are reference RGB and frozen reference masks/features.

\subsection{Coefficient Prediction}

Teacher raw coefficients $\mathbf c_g\in\mathbb{R}^{4}$ define frozen train-wardrobe statistics
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathbf c}=\frac{1}{5}\sum_g\mathbf c_g,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{c}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{5}\sum_g(\mathbf c_g-\bar{\mathbf c})^{\odot 2}},
\end{equation}
with every scale component required to be positive. The standardized target and inverse map are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf z_g=(\mathbf c_g-\bar{\mathbf c})\oslash\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{c},
\qquad
\mathbf c=\mathbf z\odot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{c}+\bar{\mathbf c}.
\end{equation}
Ours-v2 applies LayerNorm and a single Linear(4) map to $\Phi(\mathcal S_t)$, producing an unconstrained standardized coefficient. There is no tanh, attention, outfit-ID input, target input, or residual decoder in this branch.

\subsection{Deterministic Mean-Garment Initialization}

LayerNorm is initialized with unit scale and zero bias, and the coefficient map has exactly zero weight and bias. Every fresh Ours-v2 replicate therefore emits $\mathbf z=\mathbf 0$ at step zero, independent of the configured replicate seed. De-standardization maps standardized zero to the stored coefficient mean. In centered-coordinate semantics that mean is the raw coefficient origin (the frozen artifact preserves its finite-precision value), so the semantic chain is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf z=\mathbf 0
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\mathbf c=\bar{\mathbf c}\equiv\mathbf 0_{\mathrm{raw}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal R(\mathbf c)=\mu_{\mathcal R}.
\end{equation}
Thus the initial output is a \emph{mean-garment prediction}. It is not the physical zero residual and does not reproduce the base avatar. Zero-reference replacement and base-reference replacement are also distinct evaluator interventions and must not be conflated with initialization semantics.

\subsection{Canonical Composition and Animation}

The reconstructed six-channel residual is applied to the frozen canonical Gaussian state: position, log scaling, opacity logit, and spherical-harmonic channels are additive, while the axis-angle residual is converted to a quaternion and locally composed with the normalized base rotation. The selected canonical overrides are then passed to the frozen MMLP-Human state transaction and renderer. Only this downstream stage reads $(p_t,c_t)$. Target pose/camera are therefore necessary rendering conditions but are not garment-prediction inputs.

\subsection{Training Objective}

For a balanced batch of teacher targets, the current Ours-v2 contract is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{coef}}=
\operatorname{SmoothL1}(\hat{\mathbf z},\mathbf z^{\star}).
\end{equation}
No pairwise-geometry, sign-margin, absolute-pair, classification, image reconstruction, or render loss is included in the Ours-v2 coefficient optimizer. \subsection{Inference}

For one registered seen garment, inference extracts frozen F2 features from one to three valid references, pools them into $\Phi$, predicts $\hat{\mathbf z}$, restores $\hat{\mathbf c}$, and evaluates $\mathcal R(\hat{\mathbf c})=\mu_{\mathcal R}+B\hat{\mathbf c}$. The fixed avatar is then animated and rendered at the requested target pose/camera. The forward does not receive a garment ID, target RGB/mask, teacher coefficient, or teacher residual. This procedure selects within the frozen seen-garment residual span; it does not create residual components outside that span.
